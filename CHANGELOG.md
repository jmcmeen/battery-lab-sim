# Changelog

All notable changes to the Battery Lab Simulator. Format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

## [0.1.8]

### Project framing pivot

v0.1.8 reorients the project from generic battery R&D toward **consumer-electronics battery QA**, specifically phone-cell aging. Every chemistry, schedule, and dashboard claim in the repo is now positioned for that audience. The chemistries on the bench are the ones phones actually use (LCO + NMC, both with optional silicon-carbon anode variants); the schedules exercise phone-realistic patterns (step-charge fast-charge, calendar aging at elevated SOC); the walkthrough explicitly frames the two-chamber bench as a phone-cell aging study. LFP — added briefly during v0.1.8's first iteration as chamber B's chemistry — has been removed: it's not used in any modern smartphone, and its flat OCV plateau actively hurts fuel-gauge accuracy in consumer electronics.

### Fixed

- **DSN string-formatting was unsafe against credentials with reserved characters.** Every service in the workspace built its asyncpg connection string with `dsn = f"postgresql://{user}:{password}@{host}:{port}/{db}"` — 10 callsites total across `services/{ingester,parquet_export,analytics,watchdog,orchestrator}/` plus two test conftest files. That shape splits on the wrong delimiter the moment a password contains `@` (the userinfo/host separator), `:` (userinfo internal separator), `/` (authority terminator), `%` (percent-encoding sigil — silently decoded), or `#` (fragment delimiter). The repo's default `lab/lab` credentials are safe so the unit suite, integration suite, and chaos suite all passed at green — but any deployment with a generated password would have hit a DSN parse error in the best case, or silently misinterpreted the connection-string in the worst. Fixed by introducing `libs/batterylab/src/batterylab/db.py` exporting `make_dsn(user, password, host, port, db) -> str`, which percent-encodes `user` and `password` via `urllib.parse.quote(s, safe="")` ("no characters left unencoded", because credential contents are unconstrained — `quote_plus` would have been wrong, the DSN authority component is not `application/x-www-form-urlencoded`). All 10 callsites migrated; `services/{ingester,parquet_export,analytics,watchdog,orchestrator}/*/{main,healthcheck}.py` plus `tests/{integration,bench}/conftest.py`. Each service's `pyproject.toml` already declared `batterylab` as a workspace dep so no manifest edits were needed. New `tests/unit/test_db.py` (5 cases) and a `_make_channels` chemistry-arg fix in `tests/integration/conftest.py:169` that was caught by `make lint` after the `_make_channels(n)` → `_make_channels(n, chemistry)` signature change.
- **Version drift in `batterylab/__init__.py`.** The library's `__version__ = "0.1.3"` was four releases stale (pyproject.toml said 0.1.7) and a repo-wide grep confirmed nothing imported it. Deleted the constant rather than pinning it via `importlib.metadata.version("batterylab")` — the metadata-based read is conventional but only justifies its keep when someone reads it, and nobody does.
- **Ingester asyncpg pool sized for a fan-out that doesn't exist.** [services/ingester/src/ingester/main.py:168](services/ingester/src/ingester/main.py#L168) was `min_size=1, max_size=4` with a single consumer (the flush task). At ~10 MB idle session state per asyncpg connection, three of those four slots were dead weight in the 512 MB container budget. Dropped to `min_size=1, max_size=1` with a comment matching the orchestrator's explicit-reasoning style.

### Changed

- **Chemistry rework: LFP out, phone-cell chemistries in.** The chemistry library [libs/batterylab/src/batterylab/chemistry.py](libs/batterylab/src/batterylab/chemistry.py) now ships with four entries — `NMC`, `LCO`, `NMC+SiC`, `LCO+SiC` — covering the full 2×2 matrix that phone OEMs actually test against (cathode: high-nickel NMC vs. classic Lithium-Cobalt-Oxide; anode: graphite vs. silicon-carbon). The `LFP` entry added briefly in this release cycle is gone. `NMC_OCV` charges to **4.40 V** (phone-grade, bumped from EV-grade 4.20 V); `LCO_OCV` charges to **4.35 V** (classic phone cell, steeper top-of-charge than NMC). Two new `ChemistryParams` fields carry chemistry-specific safety envelopes: `v_max_mv` (NMC=4400, LCO=4350) and `thermal_runaway_c` (NMC=130 °C, LCO=150 °C — LCO trips higher but its runaway onset is more violent due to oxygen release from the layered cathode, which the ECM doesn't model). Both are pulled into the cycler safety surface automatically: `Channel.__init__` defaults `safety_v_max_mv` from `chem.v_max_mv` (the dataclass's 4500 mV stays as a sentinel for direct-construction tests), and the module-level `THERMAL_RUNAWAY_C` constant in [libs/batterylab/src/batterylab/ecm.py](libs/batterylab/src/batterylab/ecm.py) is deleted — `ECMCell.step` reads `self.chem.thermal_runaway_c` instead.
- **Silicon-carbon anode support.** Three additional `ChemistryParams` fields encode the Si-C / graphite difference: `anode` (`"graphite"` | `"silicon_carbon"`), `max_charge_c_rate` (Si-C is 1.5C; graphite is 3.0C — OEMs cap Si-C charge rate to bound mechanical fatigue from anode swelling), and `anode_swelling_factor` (multiplier on `step_throughput_lli` in [degradation.py](libs/batterylab/src/batterylab/degradation.py); 1.5 for Si-C, 1.0 for graphite — Si-C cells fade ~50 % faster per Ah of throughput due to cumulative volume-change fatigue). The two Si-C entries (`NMC+SiC`, `LCO+SiC`) clone their cathode counterparts but with a 15 % capacity premium (3.45 Ah vs. 3.0 Ah — Si-C's lithiation capacity advantage). Six new unit tests in [tests/unit/test_chemistry.py](tests/unit/test_chemistry.py) parametrized on cathode: `test_si_c_anode_charges_slower`, `test_si_c_capacity_premium_over_graphite`, `test_si_c_fades_faster_than_graphite_at_same_throughput`. The `degradation.step_throughput_lli` change is the only behavior change for non-SiC chemistries — graphite's `anode_swelling_factor=1.0` is a no-op multiplier.
- **Chemistry-vs-temperature bench matrix (LCO + NMC, not NMC + LFP).** [docker-compose.yml](docker-compose.yml) per-cycler defaults reframed: cyclers 01–08 (Chamber A, 25 °C) default to **LCO** (the baseline phone cathode — every smartphone before ~2020 and most still); cyclers 09–16 (Chamber B, 45 °C) default to **NMC** (high-nickel flagship). Per-cycler override via `CYCLER_NN_CHEMISTRY=NMC|LCO|NMC+SiC|LCO+SiC`. With the schedule-driven runtime chemistry work below, the env now serves only as a boot-time fallback — the orchestrator writes the chassis CHEMISTRY register from `schedule.chemistry` at every kickoff and the cycler rebuilds its cells to match.
- **Schedule-driven runtime chemistry via Modbus.** New chassis-level register `ChassisReg.CHEMISTRY` (10010) carries the on-wire chemistry enum (`ChemistryId.NMC=1`, `LCO=2`, `NMC_SIC=3`, `LCO_SIC=4`). `PROTOCOL_VERSION` bumped 2 → 3 — old orchestrator code reading the new map errors cleanly per the existing recipe. Cycler-side: [services/cycler/src/cycler/modbus_server.py](services/cycler/src/cycler/modbus_server.py) gains a write dispatch on the CHEMISTRY register that calls `ChassisDataBlock._set_chemistry` — reassigns every channel's `ch.cell` to a fresh `ECMCell` of the new chemistry, resets mode/setpoint/latched_error/cycle_index, updates safety_v_max_mv from the new `chem.v_max_mv`. The rebuild is a single-event-loop atomic swap (pymodbus dispatches on the asyncio loop; cell_loop and safety_loop read `ch.cell` fresh each tick), so no task lifecycle work or asyncio lock is needed. Same-chemistry writes are no-ops (preserves aging state across same-chemistry experiments); unknown chemistry ids are logged + ignored, and the mirror loop self-corrects the register on the next tick. Orchestrator-side: [services/orchestrator/src/orchestrator/executor.py](services/orchestrator/src/orchestrator/executor.py)'s `kickoff` writes the chemistry register, reads back to verify, and marks the experiment `failed` on rejection rather than silently miscommanding C-rates. New helpers `chemistry_id_for_name` / `chemistry_name_for_id` in `modbus_maps.py` with explicit-error semantics for typos. [services/orchestrator/src/orchestrator/cycler_client.py](services/orchestrator/src/orchestrator/cycler_client.py) gains `write_chassis_chemistry` and `read_chassis_chemistry`. Three new integration tests in [tests/integration/test_chassis_chemistry.py](tests/integration/test_chassis_chemistry.py) (rebuild on chemistry write; same-chemistry write is no-op; unknown id is rejected and register self-corrects). Six new unit-test cases in `test_modbus_maps.py` (chemistry id ↔ name round-trip, unknown id raises, unknown name raises) and three in `test_schedule.py` (charge rate clip for Si-C; discharge not clipped; no clip when max_charge_c_rate is None). The Si-C C-rate clip is wired through every `step_to_command` callsite (executor.kickoff, step_one_tick, _advance, plus orchestrator resume's mode-drift check) so a schedule asking for 2C on a Si-C chemistry is silently clipped to 1.5C with a chemistry-bounded setpoint.
- **Schedule library rebuilt around chemistry suffixes.** Every shipped schedule now carries a `_lco` or `_nmc` filename suffix so chemistry is visible at a glance: `demo_5cycle_{lco,nmc}.yaml`, `soak_25c_{lco,nmc}.yaml`, `cycle_life_25C_{lco,nmc}.yaml`, `soak_45c_nmc.yaml`, `soak_accelerated_nmc.yaml`, plus four new phone-specific schedules: `phone_fastcharge_lco.yaml` and `phone_fastcharge_nmc.yaml` (multi-stage CC step-charge mirroring every flagship phone's actual fast-charge profile — 2C → 1.5C → 1C, not flat CC-CV), and `phone_calendar_45c_lco.yaml` and `phone_calendar_45c_nmc.yaml` (168 h hold at elevated SOC + 45 °C, the silent killer in phone batteries left plugged in overnight in warm pockets). 12 schedules total. NMC schedules' voltage cutoffs bumped from 4.20 V to 4.40 V to match phone-grade NMC's V_max. `make demo` default is now `demo_5cycle_lco` (chamber A's default LCO, no env override needed); `make soak.start` default is `soak_25c_lco`. Chamber-A `_nmc` schedules carry a comment block warning operators to set `CYCLER_01..08_CHEMISTRY=NMC` to override the default LCO.
- **Watchdog monitor thresholds env-tunable.** Every threshold across the three watchdog monitors becomes operator-tunable. Eight new env vars: `WATCHDOG_HEARTBEAT_THRESHOLD_S` (default 10 — **hard constraint** since the chaos recipes treat 10 wall-s as canonical), `WATCHDOG_HEARTBEAT_POLL_S` (2), `WATCHDOG_CHAMBER_BREACH_BAND_C` (5.0 °C), `WATCHDOG_CHAMBER_BREACH_DURATION_S` (300 wall-s), `WATCHDOG_CHAMBER_STARTUP_GRACE_S` (60 wall-s), `WATCHDOG_CHAMBER_CHECK_PERIOD_S` (5 wall-s), `WATCHDOG_CHASSIS_POLL_SIM_S` (5.0 sim-s), `WATCHDOG_CHASSIS_STARTUP_GRACE_POLLS` (1). Plumbing follows the v0.1.7 `fleet_monitor` pattern — `watchdog/main.py` reads env into a frozen `MonitorTunables` dataclass; each monitor's loop accepts kwarg overrides with module-level `DEFAULT_*` constants as fallbacks. `ChamberStates` gains constructor kwargs so its helpers (`update_from_msg`, `grace_active`, `is_breach_sustained`) read from instance fields. Defaults preserve existing chaos / integration behavior unchanged.
- **Contributor recipes split out of CLAUDE.md into CONTRIBUTING.md.** The "Common Tasks" section moves verbatim (with v0.1.8 updates to the chemistry recipe naming the new safety-envelope fields and Si-C anode parameters) into a new "Recipes" section in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md). CLAUDE.md's "Common Tasks" header replaced with a one-line pointer; CONTRIBUTING.md's "Recipes" section opens with a pointer back to CLAUDE.md for invariants. Claude still loads invariants from CLAUDE.md on every conversation; human contributors find recipes where conventional FOSS hygiene puts them.

## [0.1.7]

### Fixed

- **Fleet trip: 507 channels failed in ~5 wall-seconds on a 512-channel soak.** Root cause was an accepted-socket leak in the cycler's Modbus TCP server — sockets accumulated faster than the kernel's `TIME_WAIT` reaper could free them, and the container hit its default 1024 soft `RLIMIT_NOFILE` after several hours. Once the limit tripped, every channel's next Modbus interaction failed in lockstep and the per-channel cycler watchdog promoted them all to `WATCHDOG_TIMEOUT` inside one ~5 s wall-window. The first visible symptom was a wall of 507 `chassis_unreachable` leaf alerts — by then half the bench was already failed and the actual cause (FD exhaustion) was buried under the downstream cascade. The dominant contributor to steady-state FD pressure turned out to be the Docker `HEALTHCHECK` itself: `cycler.healthcheck` and `chamber.healthcheck` each opened a fresh `AsyncModbusTcpClient("127.0.0.1", 502)` every 10 s, and at 16 cyclers × 6/min × hours that churn was a larger fraction of new accepts than legitimate orchestrator traffic. Fixed by inverting the healthcheck pattern: each service now runs an `alive_writer` task that `Path.touch`es `/tmp/<service>.alive` every 5 wall-seconds inside its existing `TaskGroup`, and the healthcheck `stat()`s that file and asserts mtime ≤ 30 s. Zero new sockets per probe, no Modbus pressure at all. The 30 s threshold is 6× the 5 s writer period — wide enough to ride out a saturated event loop under high `SIM_TIME_FACTOR`, tight enough that a wedged main task fails the probe within one Docker interval. New shared module `libs/batterylab/src/batterylab/alive.py` (3 functions: `_touch`, `alive_writer`, `is_alive`) is wired into both `services/cycler/src/cycler/main.py` and `services/chamber/src/chamber/main.py`; both healthcheck modules drop their `asyncio` + `pymodbus.client` imports and shrink to a one-liner `sys.exit(0 if is_alive(...) else 1)`. Wall-time on both ends because Docker's `HEALTHCHECK interval` is wall-time and has to stay correct under any `SIM_TIME_FACTOR`. Trade-off: the previous probe also asserted `PROTOCOL_VERSION` matched the library constant — a stale-image guard. That signal is gone from the healthcheck path; the orchestrator still reads `PROTOCOL_VERSION` on chassis kickoff so a mismatch surfaces as a kickoff failure rather than container-unhealthy. Acceptable because stale-image drift is a deploy-time concern, not a runtime liveness concern.
- **Docker container soft FD limit was the platform default of 1024.** Belt-and-suspenders against the failure mode above: even with the healthcheck rewrite removing the dominant FD churn, a future regression in the cycler's Modbus accept-and-cleanup path or pymodbus version bump could re-introduce a slow leak. Raised `ulimits.nofile` to `soft: 65536, hard: 65536` on the `x-cycler-base` and `x-chamber-base` YAML anchors in `docker-compose.yml`, so all 16 cyclers and 2 chambers inherit it via YAML merge — adding a 17th cycler stays a one-line change. A future leak now manifests as a slow drift visible in the `fd_pressure_high` tripwire (below) hours before it can exhaust the limit, instead of a sharp cliff at 1024.

### Added

- **FD-pressure tripwire** ([libs/batterylab/src/batterylab/fdpressure.py](libs/batterylab/src/batterylab/fdpressure.py)). New `fd_pressure_monitor` async task running in both cycler and chamber `TaskGroup`s, polling `len(os.listdir('/proc/self/fd'))` against the soft `RLIMIT_NOFILE` every 60 wall-seconds. Emits a structlog `fd_pressure_high` WARNING (with `open_fds`, `soft_limit`, `ratio`) when the ratio crosses 80 %. Pure observability — never a halt path, never an actuator path back into the service (per CLAUDE.md invariant #10). Edge-triggered re-warning every poll while the condition holds, because FD pressure is monotonically worsening and silence after the first warning would be misleading; an operator grepping the structlog stream can read the slope. Linux-only by design (`/proc/self/fd` is Linux; the function returns -1 on other platforms and the monitor short-circuits) — the production target is Linux containers, dev hosts that aren't Linux just don't get the signal. Wall-time cadence because FD pressure is a real-world resource concern, not a simulation concept.
- **Fleet-failure rollup alert** ([services/watchdog/src/watchdog/fleet_monitor.py](services/watchdog/src/watchdog/fleet_monitor.py)). New `fleet_monitor_loop` task added to the watchdog's per-session `TaskGroup`, polling Postgres every 10 s wall for `experiments` rows that flipped to `status='failed'` with `finished_at` inside a rolling 30 s window. When count ≥ threshold, emits a single `Alert(severity='critical', source='watchdog.fleet', message='mass_chassis_failure')` with `EdgeTrigger` rising-edge dedupe keyed on `('mass_chassis_failure',)` — falling edge re-arms. On a v0.1.7-scale event, on-call gets one critical alert instead of 507 leaf alerts and can recognize the *event* rather than parsing the flood. Three new env vars: `WATCHDOG_FLEET_FAIL_THRESHOLD` (default 8 — a healthy 512-channel bench should never see 8 concurrent failures in 30 s; that's 1.5 % loss inside the window), `WATCHDOG_FLEET_FAIL_WINDOW_S` (default 30), `WATCHDOG_FLEET_FAIL_POLL_S` (default 10). Polled against Postgres rather than MQTT because the orchestrator writes `status='failed'` via asyncpg when a fault trips and the `AlertSink` already holds an asyncpg pool — Postgres polling survives MQTT partitions, an MQTT-only detector wouldn't. Window-overlap is intentional: the same rolling window re-issues every poll, so a burst straddling two polls still trips on the latest poll because dedupe keeps it from re-firing while the breach persists. A transient asyncpg error returns 0 and logs `fleet_monitor_db_error` instead of raising, so the next poll retries cleanly. Plumbed through `_run_session` and `_run` in [services/watchdog/src/watchdog/main.py](services/watchdog/src/watchdog/main.py); env vars surface in the existing `watchdog_starting` log line for operability.
- **Test coverage** for all three new modules. `tests/unit/test_alive.py` (4 cases — missing file → false, recent mtime → true, stale mtime → false, `alive_writer` actually touches the path). `tests/unit/test_fdpressure.py` (3 cases — `_open_fd_count` returns positive on Linux, monitor warns when ratio exceeds threshold, monitor silent below threshold). `tests/unit/test_fleet_monitor.py` (8 cases — 5 driving `fleet_monitor_step` directly with a synthetic count: below-threshold doesn't emit, at-threshold emits critical with correct message slug, repeated breach only emits once, falling edge re-arms, zero failures silent on first call; plus 3 driving `_count_recent_failures` against a fake asyncpg pool: row-count happy path, `None` row → 0, `PostgresError` → 0). No Postgres container needed for unit-level coverage.

### Changed

- **Cycle KPIs dashboard: five "vs Cycle" panels switched from `timeseries` to `xychart`** ([grafana/dashboards/cycle_kpis.json](grafana/dashboards/cycle_kpis.json)). Symptom that drove the change: on a long soak the dashboard's time picker (default "Last 6 hours") was clipping cycle history off-axis. The panels' SQL projected `computed_at AS "time"` so Grafana plotted real time on X — but per-cycle features are computed once at cycle-completion, so a soak whose first cycle completed yesterday would show that point sitting outside the time window and the panel rendered as if cycles 1–N didn't exist. Affected panels: id 7 (Capacity vs Cycle), id 8 (Coulombic efficiency vs Cycle), id 9 (Internal resistance R₀ vs Cycle), id 11 (dQ/dV peak voltage shift vs Cycle), id 12 (SOH vs Cycle). Rewritten as `xychart` with `format: table` and X = `cycle_index`, so every recorded cycle renders regardless of where the time picker is parked. The dQ/dV panel additionally pivoted its SQL from long-format (one row per peak, `'peak_' || rn AS metric`) to wide-format (one row per cycle, `MAX(voltage_v) FILTER (WHERE rn = N) AS peak_N` for N ∈ {1, 2, 3}), because `xychart` plots each non-X column as a separate series. The "Voltage during latest cycle" panel (id 10, time-on-X by design) keeps its `timeseries` type — that one is genuinely a time trace. Each migrated panel uses `custom.show: "lines+points"`, `pointSize.fixed: 5`, `lineWidth: 2` to preserve the visual style of the previous `timeseries` panels.
- **`.env.example`** gains the three `WATCHDOG_FLEET_FAIL_*` knobs in a dedicated comment block immediately after the analytics block. Defaults match the in-code defaults so the documented surface and the runtime surface can't drift.
- **`docker-compose.yml`** `watchdog` service block gains `WATCHDOG_FLEET_FAIL_THRESHOLD`, `WATCHDOG_FLEET_FAIL_WINDOW_S`, `WATCHDOG_FLEET_FAIL_POLL_S` plumbing using the same `${VAR:-default}` pattern as the rest of the file, so `.env` overrides flow through without further edits.

## [0.1.6]

### Added

- **Chassis Overview dashboard** ([grafana/dashboards/chassis_overview.json](grafana/dashboards/chassis_overview.json), uid `chassis-overview`, refresh 5 s). Bench-wide situational awareness — sits between the per-channel `live_bench` (1 Hz, drills into one chassis × channel) and the per-experiment `cycle_kpis` (one experiment, vs cycle). The main panel is a stable 16-row table built around `WITH chassis(id) AS (SELECT generate_series(1, 16)::smallint)` plus LEFT JOINs over `experiments` (status counts via FILTER aggregates), `experiment_steps` (max cycle as `MAX(cycle_index)+1` over completed rows for *running* experiments only — the high-water mark of the current run, not the bench's lifetime), and `alerts` (24 h count). Same `generate_series` LEFT-JOIN trick `live_bench` uses for its 16 × 32 heatmap, so chassis that have gone silent still appear instead of vanishing. Columns: chassis, chamber (A 25 °C / B 45 °C, derived from `chassis_id` since 1–8 are in chamber A and 9–16 in chamber B per CLAUDE.md), the alphabetically-first running schedule, running/pending/failed/completed, max cycle, alerts. Field overrides paint `failed` > 0 red and `running` green when it hits the per-chassis target. Top strip: four stats (channels running, active schedules, telemetry freshness across chassis, critical alerts 24 h). Bottom strip: chamber A and B `temperature_c` min/avg/max time-bucketed at 30 s, so a cell drifting hotter than its peers under the same setpoint is one glance.

- **Storage dashboard** ([grafana/dashboards/storage.json](grafana/dashboards/storage.json), uid `storage`, refresh 30 s). Database-tier visibility that was previously only inferable from `make duckdb` and shell. Three stat strips: TimescaleDB hot tier (live row count from `pg_stat_user_tables.n_live_tup` rather than `COUNT(*)` — the latter full-scans the hypertable; `hypertable_size('telemetry')` for total bytes; chunk count from `timescaledb_information.chunks`; oldest-row age as the retention indicator), MinIO cold tier (driven entirely by the `parquet_exports` ledger that lives in TSDB Postgres — file count, summed `row_count`/`byte_count`, last-export age with thresholds at 2× and 4× `PARQUET_EXPORT_PERIOD_S`), and Postgres metadata (`pg_database_size('lab')` plus `n_live_tup` for `experiments`, `experiment_steps`, `cycle_features`). One full-width ingest-rate timeseries underneath, `time_bucket('1m', time)` against the telemetry hypertable — the project's 5,120 rows/s SLO floor sits implicit; gaps below it mean lost telemetry, ingester back-pressure, or a chassis dropping out. Two inventory tables at the bottom: per-chunk Timescale state with `is_compressed` color-coded green-when-true (compressed chunks are read-only per the CLAUDE.md gotcha), and the most-recent 50 `parquet_exports` rows showing `hour_start`, `exported_at`, `row_count`, `pg_size_pretty(byte_count)`, and the `s3_path` that `duckdb_init.sql` globs to build `telemetry_cold`. Refresh is 30 s rather than 5 s — storage state changes slowly, and faster polling would just hammer the system catalogs. Cold-vs-hot coverage timeline (which hours sit in hot only, cold only, or both, as a stacked-bar by time bucket) was a v2 candidate but deferred — it'd need a DuckDB Grafana datasource that doesn't exist.

### Fixed

- **Cycle KPIs dashboard showed the previous run's cycle count after re-running `make soak.start` on the same bench.** Symptom: a freshly-restarted soak with the orchestrator only a few cycles in already showed e.g. "31 Cycles completed" on the chassis × channel selector, frozen at that number until the new run eventually climbed past it — confusingly, the per-cycle telemetry plots were correctly empty/short, but the stat panel disagreed. Root cause is the deterministic experiment id pattern in `scripts/run_soak.sh` (`soak-${SCHEDULE}-c${chassis}-ch${channel}`) combined with the upsert-on-re-enrol path. The script's `INSERT INTO experiments … ON CONFLICT (id) DO UPDATE SET status='pending'` is the right shape for the stable-id resume contract (CLAUDE.md invariant #5) — but `ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE` is **not** a `DELETE`, so the FK CASCADEs on `experiment_steps` and `cycle_features` (both `REFERENCES experiments(id) ON DELETE CASCADE` per `migrations/postgres/001_metadata.sql` / `003_cycle_features.sql`) never fire. The previous run's per-cycle rows survived intact under the same `experiment_id`, and the panel's `SELECT MAX(cycle_index)+1 FROM experiment_steps WHERE experiment_id='…' AND state='completed'` (in `grafana/dashboards/cycle_kpis.json`) returned yesterday's high-water mark. Same trap exists in `scripts/run_demo.sh` (id prefix `demo-…`) — a back-to-back demo run with the same schedule + bench would have repeated the bug, just shorter-lived. Fixed in both runners by computing the set of target ids into a `TEMP TABLE … ON COMMIT DROP` before the upsert and explicitly `DELETE`-ing matching rows from `experiment_steps` and `cycle_features` first; the whole block is wrapped in `BEGIN`/`COMMIT` so an INSERT failure rolls the cleanup back. The experiments row's identity (and therefore `created_at`, the foreign key target, and the orchestrator's resume contract) is preserved — only the dependent per-cycle history is reset, which is what re-enrolling with `status='pending'` always *meant* to do per the existing comment "resets channels that previously failed/completed". To fix a bench that already has stale rows from a pre-patch run, either re-invoke `make soak.start` (the patched script will clean up on the way through) or, to leave the running soak undisturbed, delete only rows above the current high-water mark — the conservative form `DELETE … WHERE cycle_index > <highest_in_current_run>` avoids the trap of clearing the rows the orchestrator's `_load_resume_state` reads to find its position on next restart.

- **Overnight soaks filled the host disk (1.8 TB observed) without growing any project volume.** Confusing diagnostic surface: `docker compose down` (without `-v`) reclaimed almost all of it, which looked like volume-resident data but isn't — `tsdb_data`, `pg_data`, `minio_data`, `grafana_data` are all named volumes that survive `down`. Root cause was unbounded container stdout/stderr logs. The host has no `/etc/docker/daemon.json`, so the daemon defaults applied: `json-file` driver, no `max-size`, no rotation. Each container writes to `/var/lib/docker/containers/<id>/<id>-json.log` and grows the file until the disk is full; `docker compose down` deletes the containers and those log files come with them, which exactly matches the "free on down, no volume loss" symptom. The 512-channel bench is a worst-case workload for this default — 16 chassis × 32 channels × 10 Hz ≈ 5,120 telemetry rows/s, every service emits structlog JSON, and the orchestrator/ingester/watchdog all chatter continuously. Overnight × 28 active containers × no rotation ≈ TB-class output. Fixed by adding an `x-logging: &default-logging` anchor to `docker-compose.yml` (`json-file` + `max-size: ${LOG_MAX_SIZE:-50m}` + `max-file: ${LOG_MAX_FILE:-5}`) and applying it to `x-cycler-base`, `x-chamber-base`, and the 10 standalone service blocks — 28 total. The two anchors cover all 16 cyclers and both chambers via YAML merge, so adding a 17th cycler stays a one-line change. `duckdb_cli` is intentionally excluded: it's `profiles: ["cli"]`, interactive (`stdin_open`/`tty`), and you want its stdout un-truncated when you're driving it from `make duckdb.query`. Worst-case footprint with the new defaults is `50m × 5 × 28 ≈ 7 GB`, comfortably below any developer laptop. Two new env vars (`LOG_MAX_SIZE`, `LOG_MAX_FILE`) added to `.env` and `.env.example` so a longer-retention forensic run is one edit, not a compose change. Reclaiming the 1.8 TB on existing benches is `make down && make up` — `down` deletes the giant files, `up` brings everything back with rotation in force, volumes untouched.

## [0.1.5]

### Fixed

- **`make parquet.export.now` (and the periodic loop) OOM-killed at 512 MB on a full bench.** A closed hour at 16 chassis × 32 channels × 10 Hz produces ~16-18M telemetry rows. The previous `fetch_hour` did `await conn.fetch(...)` (materializing every row as an asyncpg `Record`), then pivoted into a `dict[str, list]` (~28 bytes/value × 10 columns × 16M rows ≈ 4 GB of Python-object overhead), then handed it to `pa.Table.from_pydict`. The container's 512 MB limit was reached during the dict pivot, well before Arrow ever got a chance to compact, and the kernel sent SIGKILL — surfaced as `make: *** Error 137` with no stderr because the OOM kill is silent. Bumping `mem_limit` would have just kicked the can to 4 GB; instead, `services/parquet_export/src/parquet_export/export.py` now streams. `export_hour` opens an asyncpg `cursor` inside a transaction, fetches `_BATCH_ROWS = 100_000` records at a time, pivots each batch into a `pa.RecordBatch`, and writes via `pq.ParquetWriter.write_batch` to a single S3 multipart upload. Peak in-flight memory is bounded by the batch size (~10 MB of Records + ~2 MB of Arrow buffers) regardless of how many rows the hour holds, so the same code path handles a 60-row test hour and an 18M-row production hour. The ParquetWriter is opened lazily on the first non-empty batch — empty hours leave no S3 object behind and are still recorded as a sentinel ledger row in `parquet_exports`, preserving the "don't re-scan empty hours" invariant. The previous `fetch_hour` and `write_parquet` helpers are removed (they were only ever called by `export_hour`); the public `TELEMETRY_SCHEMA`, `record_export`, `find_pending_hours`, `drop_exported_chunks`, and `hour_floor` surface is unchanged, so `main.run_once` and the test suite need no edits.
- **`scripts/duckdb_entrypoint.sh` cold-tier probe duplicated S3 config (endpoint, bucket path, credentials) with `scripts/duckdb_init.sql`.** Drift between the two would have produced a probe that disagreed with the templated init — e.g. moving the cold tier to a new MinIO endpoint required editing the entrypoint *and* the init SQL, with no test that caught the second edit being skipped. Centralized: `MINIO_ENDPOINT` (default `minio:9000`) and a derived `COLD_GLOB = s3://${PARQUET_BUCKET}/telemetry/**/*.parquet` are computed once at the top of the entrypoint and exported into the envsubst pass that renders the init SQL. `scripts/duckdb_init.sql` now reads `${MINIO_ENDPOINT}` and `${COLD_GLOB}` instead of literal strings. The `duckdb_cli` compose service block gains `MINIO_ENDPOINT: minio:9000` and `PARQUET_BUCKET: ${PARQUET_BUCKET:-lab-archive}` so its values match `parquet_export`'s — a relocated cold tier is now one `.env` change instead of three across files.
- **DuckDB credential interpolation could break on a single-quote in `MINIO_ROOT_USER`/`MINIO_ROOT_PASSWORD` or the TSDB equivalents.** DuckDB string literals are single-quoted, and our SQL templates the credentials inside `'...'` in two places (the inline probe and the post-envsubst init). A stray `'` in any credential would parse-error or, worse, silently truncate. Doubling-the-quote escaping would have to be applied in both places (envsubst doesn't transform values) and there's no upstream library for "DuckDB-quote a string", so we refuse the input instead — the entrypoint fails fast with `duckdb_init: credentials must not contain single quotes` before envsubst runs. Local-dev `changeme` defaults are unaffected; deployments with stranger credentials get a clear error at container start instead of an opaque DuckDB parse failure halfway through init.
- **DuckDB cold-tier probe silently treated any duckdb failure as "no cold data".** The previous shape was `COLD_FILES=$(duckdb ... 2>/dev/null || echo 0)`, which mapped a misconfiguration (bad endpoint, bad creds, network blip) to "0 files" — identical to the legitimate empty-bench case. The cold block was stripped, `telemetry_cold` and `telemetry_all` disappeared, and the operator had no way to tell whether the cold tier was empty or unreachable. Now the entrypoint captures stdout and stderr separately and branches on duckdb's exit status: a clean `0` count still prints the existing `(cold tier empty; …)` notice, but a non-zero exit prints `WARNING: cold-tier probe failed; falling back to hot-only.` followed by the indented duckdb stderr. We still degrade to hot-only (so the operator can keep querying `telemetry_hot`), but the WARNING surfaces the actual failure mode for triage.

## [0.1.4]

### Fixed

- **`make duckdb` / `make duckdb.query` had three independent failure modes that compounded into "HTTP 403 against `s3://lab-archive`".** Surface symptom was a LIST 403; root cause was a stack of broken assumptions in `scripts/duckdb_init.sql` and the `duckdb_cli` compose service. All three are now fixed together — but worth recording each so the same mistakes don't reappear.
  - **Credentials hardcoded in init.sql.** `scripts/duckdb_init.sql` baked `user=lab password=lab` for the TimescaleDB `ATTACH` and `admin / admin12345` for the MinIO `s3_*` settings, while `.env.example` (and the user's `.env`) ship `TSDB_PASSWORD=changeme` and `MINIO_ROOT_PASSWORD=changeme`. Anyone following the standard setup got a SigV4 mismatch on the first MinIO call and an `auth failed` on the first hot-tier query. Fixed by templating the init script — placeholders (`${TSDB_*}`, `${MINIO_ROOT_*}`) are expanded by a new `scripts/duckdb_entrypoint.sh` wrapper using `envsubst` (restricted to the five vars we template, so future literal `$` strings in the SQL don't get clobbered). Required-var checks (`: "${VAR:?...}"`) at the top of the entrypoint fail fast with a clear message instead of silently passing through empty creds. DuckDB v1.1.3 accepts `getenv()` in `SET` but rejects it inside the `ATTACH` connection-string literal, which is why we template the file rather than use `getenv()` per-setting.
  - **`make duckdb.query Q=...` was never loading the init script in the first place.** The Makefile target runs `docker compose run --rm -T duckdb_cli -c "$(Q)"`. Compose treats those trailing args as a *replacement* for the service's `command:`, not an append — so the `["-init", "/scripts/duckdb_init.sql"]` command was discarded entirely, and DuckDB started with no httpfs, no `s3_endpoint`, and no `ATTACH`. The original 403 was DuckDB sending an unauthenticated LIST to **real AWS S3** (the default `s3.amazonaws.com` endpoint), not MinIO. Fixed by baking the templating + init into the image's `ENTRYPOINT`: `scripts/duckdb_entrypoint.sh` is `COPY`'d to `/usr/local/bin/duckdb-init` and set as the entrypoint, so `docker compose run … -c "SQL"` now resolves to `/usr/local/bin/duckdb-init -c "SQL"` — the wrapper renders the init script, runs the cold-tier probe, then `exec`s `duckdb -init /tmp/duckdb_init.sql "$@"` with the user's args appended. Both `make duckdb` (interactive) and `make duckdb.query Q=…` paths run identical setup.
  - **Empty cold bucket blocked init entirely.** On a fresh bench (or any state where `parquet_export` hasn't shipped its first archive — closed hours older than `PARQUET_EXPORT_AGE_HOURS`, default 24h), `read_parquet('s3://lab-archive/telemetry/**/*.parquet')` errors at *view-creation* time with "No files found", which made the `telemetry_cold` view fail, which cascaded into `telemetry_all` failing with a "Did you mean 'telemetry_hot'?" catalog error — leaving the user with a noisy stack of errors and no usable views. `read_parquet` doesn't have an "allow empty" option, but `glob()` returns 0 rows cleanly when nothing matches. Wrapped the cold-tier views in `-- BEGIN_COLD --` / `-- END_COLD --` markers; the entrypoint runs a one-line `glob('s3://...')` probe via a tiny duckdb invocation and sed-strips that block when the count is 0. On an empty bench, init prints `(cold tier empty; telemetry_cold/telemetry_all skipped — telemetry_hot is available)` and `telemetry_hot` works as expected. Once `parquet_export` ships the first archive, the probe finds files on the next invocation and the cold/all views come back automatically — no rebuild, no reconfig.
- **`scripts/duckdb_cli.Dockerfile`** gains `gettext-base` (for `envsubst`) in the existing apt install line, and `COPY duckdb_entrypoint.sh /usr/local/bin/duckdb-init` plus an `ENTRYPOINT` switch from `["duckdb"]` to `["/usr/local/bin/duckdb-init"]`. Image size delta is ~200 KB.
- **`docker-compose.yml`** `duckdb_cli` service block gains `environment:` plumbing for `TSDB_USER`, `TSDB_PASSWORD`, `TSDB_DB`, `MINIO_ROOT_USER`, `MINIO_ROOT_PASSWORD` — same `${VAR:-default}` substitution pattern the rest of the file uses, so `.env` overrides flow through without further edits. The previous compose-level `command: ["-init", …]` is dropped (the entrypoint owns init now).

## [0.1.3]

### Added

- **Per-channel watchdog keepalive** — new `services/orchestrator/src/orchestrator/channel_keepalive.py` module + task in the orchestrator's TaskGroup. Mirrors the existing chassis-level `heartbeat_loop`, but at channel granularity: every running channel's per-channel dead-man register is written every 1 sim-sec, fanning out across chassis via `asyncio.gather` (each chassis's writes serialize on its single Modbus connection, but chassis run truly in parallel). Industry-standard split between the *command path* and the *safety keepalive path* — same pattern Arbin/Maccor/BioLogic use to decouple safety heartbeating from command issuance latency. CLAUDE.md invariant #1 still holds: this loop only writes the kick register; the cycler is the safety actuator. If the loop dies, the per-channel watchdog trips within 5 s wall and channels halt — exactly as designed. New unit test `tests/unit/test_orchestrator_channel_keepalive.py` (5 cases — running-only filter, unknown-chassis skip, OSError isolation, cross-chassis parallelism shape, single-pass dispatch).

### Fixed

- **Kickoff race tripped 256-channel benches at startup.** On a full soak (e.g. `soak_45c` enrolling chassis 9-16 × 32 channels), the orchestrator's executor loop iterated all 256 pending experiments serially, calling `kickoff` for each. With ~5 Modbus writes + 2 DB writes per kickoff and ~30 ms per Modbus roundtrip, the full sweep took ~8 s wall — exceeding the cycler's 5 s wall-time per-channel watchdog. Channels kicked first sat non-idle without re-kick long enough to latch `WATCHDOG_TIMEOUT`; on the next tick the orchestrator read `last_error` and flipped them to `failed`. Symptom: 23/32 channels per chassis failed at startup with the Cycle KPIs dashboard showing `failed` while telemetry kept flowing (cyclers are independent of the orchestrator's metadata DB per CLAUDE.md invariant #1). Fixed by fanning out `_executor_loop`'s kickoff and tick passes per-chassis via `asyncio.gather` — each chassis processes its own 32 channels serially (the connection serializes anyway), but cross-chassis parallelism cuts the worst-case sweep to one chassis's 32 channels (~1 s wall) instead of all 256. Same restructure applied to `_resume_inflight` so a 60-hour-soak resume doesn't burn its 5 s chassis-dead-man budget on serial Modbus.
- **Resume race halted previously-running channels on orchestrator restart.** In `services/orchestrator/src/orchestrator/main.py:_run`, the master-arming `c.kick_chassis()` call ran *after* `_resume_inflight`. By the time the orchestrator booted, every chassis dead-man was past its 5 s threshold (cycler-boot + orchestrator cold-start), so the resume drift handler's `send_command` would transition channels from `idle` → `cc` while the chassis was still tripped — and the next iteration of the cycler's `chassis_watchdog` (running every ~1.7 ms wall) would halt the channel with `WATCHDOG_TIMEOUT`. Resume read happened *before* the halt, so it logged `experiment_resumed`, then the very next executor tick saw `last_error` and flipped the experiment to `failed`. Reordered so chassis kicks happen before resume: chassis dead-man timers are fresh when drift handlers fire.
- **`asyncpg` pool sized for per-chassis fan-out.** Bumped `min_size=1, max_size=8` to `min_size=1, max_size=16` so 16 chassis processing kickoffs / ticks / resumes in parallel don't queue on connection acquisition. Each chassis's coroutine holds at most one connection at a time (DB writes serialize within `kickoff` / `step_one_tick`), so 16 is right-sized to `MAX_CHASSIS`.
- **Per-chassis sweep failures no longer tear down the whole bench.** All four fan-out gather sites (`_kickoff_pending`, `_tick_running`, `_resume_inflight`, `channel_keepalive_loop`) used `asyncio.gather(..., return_exceptions=False)`, so a single unexpected exception in one chassis's coroutine — anything past the per-experiment `OSError` guards: a Pydantic dataclass instantiation error, asyncpg pool exhaustion, an internal invariant violation — would cancel every other chassis's in-flight sweep. Switched all four to `return_exceptions=True` and added a shared `_log_chassis_sweep_errors` helper that logs the offending chassis + sweep name and lets the rest of the bench keep progressing. `asyncio.CancelledError` is re-raised so shutdown still propagates correctly.
- **`_resume_inflight` no longer mutates a shared dict from gathered tasks.** The previous implementation accumulated resumed experiments by writing into a single closure-captured `out` dict from N concurrent per-chassis coroutines. Safe in CPython's single-threaded event loop today, but a forward-portability hazard. Refactored each `resume_one_chassis` to return its own private dict; the caller merges results after `gather` finishes. Same observable behaviour, fewer implicit assumptions.

### Tests

- **`tests/unit/test_orchestrator_channel_keepalive.py` made deterministic.** The `_run_one_iteration` helper previously yielded with `for _ in range(20): await asyncio.sleep(0)` and then cancelled the keepalive task — heuristic, coupled to the loop's exact internal `await` count, and a flake risk if the implementation grew another await. Replaced with a monkeypatched `channel_keepalive.SimTime.sleep` that sets an `asyncio.Event` on each end-of-sweep call; the helper waits on that event with a 5 s timeout and cancels exactly once. `test_chassis_run_in_parallel` similarly stopped asserting on event-loop interleaving (the previous version recorded the `(chassis_id, channel_idx)` order and checked specific cross-chassis ordering, which is implementation-defined under the asyncio scheduler) — switched to an `asyncio.Barrier(parties=2)` where each chassis's first kick blocks on the barrier; a parallel gather releases both, a serial gather deadlocks the helper's `wait_for` and the test fails fast. No production behaviour change; the tests now assert what they actually mean.

## [0.1.0] — initial release

The full system as described in the [README](README.md): 16 simulated cyclers × 32 channels = 512 cells driven by a YAML-scheduled orchestrator, hot/cold telemetry tiers (TimescaleDB → Parquet on MinIO), streaming cycle analytics, a watchdog with durable alerting, three provisioned Grafana dashboards, and a chaos-engineering regression suite.

### Added

**Simulation core**
- ECM cell physics (1st-order RC + thermal coupling), calibrated for NMC and LFP chemistries. Pure-function math in `libs/batterylab/ecm.py` and `chemistry.py`.
- 100 Hz cycler safety loop with wall-clock dead-man timer, running inside each cycler container — `V_max`, `T_max`, and watchdog enforcement live in hardware, never in the orchestrator (CLAUDE.md invariant #1).
- Multi-channel cycler service: channel-addressed Modbus TCP map, asyncio-per-channel control law (cc/cv/cp/rest), MQTT telemetry at `TELEMETRY_HZ`. One container per chassis, N channels as asyncio tasks (CLAUDE.md invariant #2).
- Thermal chamber service with first-order thermal dynamics (`tau_s` configurable). One container per chamber.

**Control plane**
- YAML-scheduled orchestrator with Pydantic schema validation. Every `experiments` row records the schedule's git SHA — full row-to-commit reproducibility.
- Idempotent commands and idempotent resume: on boot, re-issue the expected command (warning on drift); fail-and-alarm only on persistent drift across two resume attempts in one process lifetime (CLAUDE.md invariant #5, #9).
- Per-channel and per-chassis Modbus dead-man timers, kept on wall time so they're independent of `SIM_TIME_FACTOR`.
- Retained `experiment/<chassis>/<channel>` MQTT topic carries `(schedule_id, step_name, step_index, cycle_index)` — Sparkplug-B / device-shadow pattern, ingester joins onto telemetry rows at parse time.

**Storage**
- Hot tier: TimescaleDB hypertable, 1-hour chunks, native compression after 24 h, 1-second continuous aggregate.
- Cold tier: hourly zstd Parquet on MinIO, Hive-partitioned (`year/month/day/hour`). Idempotent exporter tracks every exported hour in `parquet_exports` and only drops underlying TSDB chunks once every covered hour is in S3.
- Cross-tier queries via DuckDB — one SQL session reads both tiers through a `telemetry_all` UNION view (`scripts/duckdb_init.sql`).
- Strict separation of metadata (Postgres) from telemetry (TimescaleDB) per CLAUDE.md invariant #3.
- Schema reference: [docs/SCHEMA.md](docs/SCHEMA.md).

**Telemetry pipeline**
- MQTT → TimescaleDB ingester using `COPY` (50× faster than `INSERT` at 5,120 rows/sec sustained), with a 50k-row hard buffer cap that drops oldest on back-pressure (at-most-once, matching QoS 0).

**Reliability**
- Watchdog service with heartbeat liveness monitor, chassis dead-man poller, and chamber temperature drift detector. Writes durable `alerts` rows; per CLAUDE.md invariant #10, never reaches back into the cycler.
- `EdgeTrigger` alert deduplication, keyed by `(message, chassis_id, channel_idx)`, so a sustained condition doesn't spam the alert log.

**Analytics**
- Streaming cycle features computed on every `events/cycle_complete`: capacity (Coulomb counting), Coulombic efficiency, peak T, R₀ from CC→CV transition, dQ/dV peaks (Severson 2019). One row per `(experiment_id, cycle_index)` in the `cycle_features` table.
- R₀-jump anomaly detection writes `analytics.anomaly` alerts when relative jump exceeds `ANALYTICS_R0_JUMP_THRESHOLD_PCT` (default 20 %).

**Dashboards (auto-provisioned)**
- **Live Bench** (1 s refresh) — V/T/SOC heatmaps for all 512 channels, ingest rate, active-channel count.
- **Cycle KPIs** (5 s) — voltage trajectory, step durations, capacity / CE / R₀ vs cycle, dQ/dV peaks (latest cycle table + per-cycle peak-shift line plot), SOH vs cycle.
- **Reliability** (5 s) — alert log, telemetry freshness, watchdog trips by chassis, R₀-jump anomaly log.

**Chaos engineering**
- `chaos/powerfail.sh` — keystone: kills the orchestrator mid-cycle, asserts safe trip + clean resume.
- `chaos/kill_cycler.sh`, `chaos/kill_db.sh` — blast-radius and DB-reconnect proofs.
- `chaos/partition_orchestrator.sh`, `chaos/flap_network.sh` — `tc netem` scenarios; demo-only because timing is too flaky for CI.
- `tests/chaos/` — pytest harness; `make test.chaos` runs the automated subset in ~90 s wall against a live `make up` stack.
